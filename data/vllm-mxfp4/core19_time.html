Speedup rises with task length

The microbenchmarks predicted this: prefill is 3.2–3.6× faster on this stack and decode ~23% slower, so tasks that accumulate context gain, while short decode-bound tasks lose a little.

The numbers

Reading it: the two MXFP4 arms total 241.5 and 240.8 agent-minutes against llama.cpp's 378.1 — both exactly 1.57×, and 0.997× of each other. The engine-and-format change buys the wall clock; the weights buy none of it. The speedup is a slope, not a constant: the longest baseline tasks run 2.0–3.8× faster while tasks under ~6 baseline minutes sit at or below parity, because an agent re-prefills its whole context every step and prefill is where this stack wins. mailman is the divergence — heretic 17.0 min and a pass, stock 36.6 min and a fail, then a retry that hit ContextWindowExceededError at 65,537 tokens against the 65,536 limit this run was configured with and burned the 3-hour ceiling (218.5 min total). That limit was our choice, not the card's: 131,072 serves on this hardware without the drafter (MISTAKES), so the heretic-vs-stock gap is softer than the scores suggest. extract-elf and configure-git-webserver are llama.cpp failures that both MXFP4 arms pass, so those baseline bars are time spent losing.
