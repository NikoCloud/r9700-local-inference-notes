Speedup rises with task length

The microbenchmarks predicted this: prefill is 3.2–3.6× faster on this stack and decode ~23% slower, so tasks that accumulate context gain, while short decode-bound tasks lose a little.

The numbers

Reading it: the speedup is a slope, not a constant. The four longest baseline tasks run 2.0–3.8× faster; tasks under ~6 baseline minutes are at or below parity. Totals: 240.7 min vs 378.1 min, or 1.57× — faster on 8 tasks, slower on 10, parity on 1. Both columns are agent minutes on attempt 1 (container build and verifier excluded from both). mailman is the extreme: 68.6 → 17.0 min, solved in 27 steps at a 33,610-token peak against the baseline’s 89,089 — so the 65k window never bound, and nothing summarised (max peak across all 19 was 53,727). extract-elf and configure-git-webserver are baseline failures; we pass both, so those baseline bars are time spent losing.
